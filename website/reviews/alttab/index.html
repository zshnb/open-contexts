AltTab review: free features, Pro and who it suits

AltTab is worth evaluating if you want individual windows with visual previews rather than the native app-only switcher. Its free core already handles that job; Pro adds search and extra styles. This review explains the documented tradeoffs and what to test before paying.

Written by the Open Contexts project using the competitor’s official feature and pricing documentation. This is a documentation-based review, without hands-on testing or performance benchmarks.

Where AltTab is a good fit

AltTab’s strongest case is visual recognition. Its thumbnails and selected-window preview can help distinguish windows whose titles look alike. It also documents minimized and hidden windows, Space and screen filtering, and controls to close or minimize windows from the switcher.

If you prefer a trackpad, gesture switching is included in the free version. The official feature page also lists VoiceOver, sticky keys and reduced-transparency support. These are useful reasons to try it on your own setup rather than assuming a basic keyboard switcher is enough.

What the paid upgrade adds

Search, title and app-icon styles, auto-sizing thumbnails and up to nine independent shortcuts are Pro features. Search may be the important upgrade if you have many windows and remember their names better than their appearance. A title-only layout is also available in Pro, so AltTab is not limited to a thumbnail grid.

Pro is listed at $9.99 once with current-major-version updates. Pro Lifetime is $24.99 once with future updates. Each license supports three Macs. A 14-day Pro trial lets you evaluate paid features while the core features remain free afterward; confirm regional amounts at checkout.

Where to look at another workflow

A switcher is not automatically a project organizer. If your main goal is named, saved groups and manual window ordering in a persistent app bar, evaluate Open Contexts. It is free but currently has no search, thumbnail grid or gesture switching.

For individual browser-tab navigation, inspect a tab-oriented tool such as Better Window Switcher. A window title is not the same as a list of all tabs in that window. This review does not claim AltTab is slower or consumes more power; no performance benchmark was run.

A useful trial checklist

  • Check the official macOS 12+ requirement and install on your actual Intel or Apple silicon Mac.
  • Quit other global window switchers, then test your preferred shortcut and reverse switching.
  • Open several similar windows and see whether thumbnails or the Pro title layout is easier for you.
  • Try minimized, hidden, multi-Space and multi-display windows that occur in your work.
  • Use the Pro trial to compare search with cycling; disable paid features before judging the long-term free experience.
  • If accessibility features matter, test them with your own assistive settings.

Is AltTab worth trying?

Yes, if the problem is choosing a specific window by its appearance: the free core offers a direct way to evaluate that workflow. Pay for Pro only if search, styles or the additional shortcut configurations solve a recurring need.

If a list plus saved task groups is what you are after, compare Open Contexts alongside it. Choose based on how you find windows during real work, rather than a star rating invented from a feature list.
